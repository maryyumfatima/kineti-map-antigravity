# Clinical Knowledge: Lower Back / Lumbar Spine

> **Purpose.** Reference knowledge loaded by the KinetiMap SOAP AI when a patient's
> primary complaint maps to the lower back / lumbar region. The AI uses it to (a)
> understand the region and (b) prompt the physiotherapist about what their
> documentation may be missing. This file informs SUGGESTIONS ONLY. The AI must
> never present anything here as a finding that occurred in the session. The
> treating physiotherapist always has the final word.
>
> Clinical content reviewed against Magee, *Orthopedic Physical Assessment*,
> 6th ed., Chapter 9 (Lumbar Spine). Assessment content is textbook-supported.
> The PLAN section is intentionally light and is NOT from Magee (an assessment
> text); it offers broad categories only and must not be treated as a treatment
> protocol.

---

## Region Scope
Lumbar spine (L1–L5) and the L5–S1 junction, including facet (zygapophyseal)
joints, intervertebral discs, ligaments, and the lumbar nerve roots. The most
common presenting complaint in physiotherapy. Because lumbar, sacroiliac, and hip
problems can refer to one another, the AI may remind the physio that the hip and
SI joints may also warrant screening when the picture is unclear.

---

## CLASSIFICATION FRAMEWORK — Hall's Four Patterns of Back Pain
A non-diagnostic way to frame a presentation. The AI may suggest which pattern the
*documented* findings appear consistent with — using "may be consistent with",
never "the patient has".

- **Pattern 1 — Back-dominant, worse on flexion, eased by extension.** Often
  associated with disc involvement. Onset hours to days; morning stiffness common.
- **Pattern 2 — Back-dominant, worse on extension/rotation, eased by flexion.**
  Often associated with facet joint involvement.
- **Pattern 3 — Leg-dominant pain, usually below the knee, worse on flexion.**
  Often associated with nerve root irritation (commonly disc-related).
- **Pattern 4 — Leg pain brought on by walking, eased by sitting/rest.** Often
  associated with neurogenic claudication (spinal stenosis).

## SUBJECTIVE — what the physio should have asked / documented
If the transcript does not clearly cover these, the AI may prompt them.

- **Age** — different conditions cluster by age (e.g. disc problems commonly 15–40;
  osteoarthritis/spondylosis more common over 45). Age is also a red-flag factor
  (see below).
- **Occupation and activity level** — strenuous work, driving (vibration),
  repetitive loading; current work status.
- **Mechanism of onset** — trauma, lifting, twisting, bending, prolonged posture,
  or gradual/insidious onset.
- **Duration** — acute = roughly 3–4 weeks; subacute = up to about 12 weeks;
  chronic = longer than about 3 months.
- **Site and boundaries of pain** — localised vs general; ask the patient to point.
- **Radiation, and centralization vs peripheralization** — is pain moving toward
  the midline/back (centralizing — generally favourable) or out into the limb
  (peripheralizing)? Pain radiating BELOW THE KNEE is highly suggestive of nerve
  root involvement / disc lesion. IMPORTANT: pain that stays in the back or buttock
  does NOT rule out a disc — a minor protrusion may produce only localised pain.
  The AI must never treat "no leg pain" as "no disc".
- **Pain type** — deep, superficial, shooting, burning, aching.
- **Pain behaviour over 24 hours** — better/worse with movement, rest, sitting,
  standing, walking; night pain; pain waking the patient.
- **Morning stiffness** — marked or prolonged morning stiffness may point toward an
  inflammatory cause (e.g. ankylosing spondylitis). Brief start-up stiffness that
  eases with movement is more typical of mechanical or facet osteoarthritic pain.
  (Magee does not specify a minute threshold; do not state one as fact.)
- **Effect of coughing/sneezing/straining** — increased pain with these raises
  intrathecal pressure and points toward neurological involvement.
- **Aggravating and easing positions/activities.**
- **Paraesthesia or numbness** — including in the perineal/saddle area (see red
  flags).
- **Bladder/bowel function** — any difficulty (see red flags).
- **Previous episodes** — first episode or recurrent; previous treatment/response.
- **Medication** — note relevant medication; long-term steroid use is relevant to
  bone health.
- **Current pain score** (e.g. NPRS 0–10) and functional impact.

### RED FLAGS — must be prompted if not documented
Screening prompts only — never a diagnosis. If present, the appropriate action is
escalation / onward referral, which is the physio's clinical decision.

**Possible serious spinal pathology / inflammatory:**
- First presentation under 20 years, or new onset over 55 years
- Violent trauma (e.g. fall from height, road traffic accident)
- Constant, progressive, non-mechanical pain
- Thoracic pain
- Previous history of carcinoma, systemic steroids, drug abuse, HIV
- Unexplained weight loss
- Systemically unwell / fever
- Persisting severe restriction of lumbar movement
- Structural deformity
- Marked morning stiffness with limitation of movement in all directions
  (inflammatory pattern)

**Cauda equina syndrome — treat as an emergency:**
- Saddle (perineal/genital/anal) anaesthesia
- Bladder or bowel dysfunction; loss of anal sphincter tone; faecal incontinence
- Bilateral leg symptoms, or widespread/progressive motor weakness in the legs;
  gait disturbance

### YELLOW FLAGS — psychosocial barriers to recovery
The AI may prompt whether psychosocial factors have been considered:
- Belief that pain and activity are harmful
- "Sickness behaviours" such as extended rest
- Low/negative mood, social withdrawal
- Compensation or claim issues; time off work
- Problems at work, poor job satisfaction
- Overprotective family or lack of support
Validated tools used in lumbar assessment include the Fear-Avoidance Beliefs
Questionnaire (FABQ) and the Linton Acute Low Back Pain Screening Questionnaire.

## OBJECTIVE — what the physio should have measured / documented
The AI may prompt for any of these if absent from the transcript.

- **Observation / posture** — antalgic posture, loss of lumbar lordosis, lateral
  shift or list, scoliosis, step deformity (possible spondylolisthesis).
- **Active lumbar range of movement**, with approximate normal ranges for
  reference: flexion 40–60°, extension 20–35°, side flexion 15–20°, rotation 3–18°.
  Note range, pain, and any painful arc or instability "jog".
- **Resisted isometric / muscle endurance** — trunk flexor and extensor strength;
  endurance tests exist (e.g. dynamic abdominal endurance, double straight leg
  lowering, side bridge, Biering-Sørensen extensor test) if relevant.
- **Neurological screen** — prompt as standard; emphasise more strongly when leg
  radiation or paraesthesia is reported. Covers:
  - **Myotomes:** L2 hip flexion, L3 knee extension, L4 ankle dorsiflexion,
    L5 great toe extension, S1 ankle plantarflexion/eversion and hip extension,
    S2 knee flexion. Hold each resisted contraction at least 5 seconds.
  - **Dermatomes and reflexes** for the relevant lumbar/sacral roots.
- **Special / neurodynamic tests** — Straight Leg Raise (record positive/negative
  and the angle of symptom reproduction), Slump test, and others as indicated.
- **Peripheral joint scan** — screen SI, hip, knee, ankle/foot when referral is
  suspected.
- **Palpation** — tenderness, level, soft tissue findings, muscle spasm.
- **Functional assessment** — effect on standing, walking, bending, lifting,
  dressing, daily activities; an outcome measure where appropriate.

> **Contraindication flag the AI should surface:** manipulation and traction are
> contraindicated where there are S3/S4 signs or bilateral sciatica from massive
> posterior disc displacement. This is a safety prompt, not a treatment instruction.

## ASSESSMENT — how the AI should frame suggestions
The AI may suggest framing and considerations; it must NOT state a diagnosis.
Acceptable language: "consider", "differential to consider", "the documented
findings may be consistent with". Never "the patient has".

- Suggest which of **Hall's four patterns** the documented findings may fit.
- Broad categories to consider: mechanical / non-specific low back pain; facet
  joint involvement; nerve root / radicular involvement; neurogenic claudication
  (stenosis); inflammatory presentation; or a presentation requiring onward
  referral.
- Prompt whether **red flags** have been screened and documented.
- Prompt whether **yellow flags** (psychosocial factors) have been considered,
  especially in subacute/chronic presentations.
- Note **irritability and severity** — how easily symptoms are provoked and how
  long they take to settle — as this guides how vigorous assessment/treatment
  should be.

## PLAN — broad categories only (NOT from Magee; not a treatment protocol)
Magee is an assessment text and does not cover treatment. This section gives the
AI only broad, non-prescriptive categories to prompt. The AI must NOT suggest
specific exercises, protocols, dosages, or named techniques here.

The AI may prompt the physio to consider documenting:
- Patient education and reassurance; advice to stay active within tolerance.
- Exercise therapy (as a category — not a specific protocol).
- Manual therapy as an adjunct (as a category).
- Activity and load modification advice.
- Whether a home exercise programme was issued.
- A review timeframe / when to reassess.
- An escalation/referral trigger — see below.

**Referral / escalation trigger:** escalate on any red flag, on progressive
neurological deficit, or on lack of expected progress measured against documented
outcome measures. (No fixed session count is mandated here; a clinic may set its
own default review point.)

## OUTCOME MEASURES relevant to this region
Standardised measures the AI may prompt the physio to record or re-administer:
- **NPRS** — Numeric Pain Rating Scale (0–10)
- **PSFS** — Patient Specific Functional Scale
- **Oswestry Disability Index** — lumbar-specific functional disability
- **Roland-Morris Disability Questionnaire** — lumbar functional disability
- **FABQ / Linton Acute LBP Screening Questionnaire** — psychosocial / fear-avoidance
(Magee's lumbar chapter references the above. STarT Back is not used in this text.)

---

## SESSION-TO-SESSION PROMPTS
When a previous note exists, the AI may surface continuity prompts such as:
- Comparing the documented pain score with the previous session.
- Asking whether the home exercise programme from last session was reviewed for
  compliance.
- Flagging when several sessions have passed with no documented measurable change.
- Prompting outcome-measure re-administration at sensible intervals.
- Noting whether pain is centralizing (generally favourable) or peripheralizing.

## HARD RULES FOR THE AI WHEN USING THIS FILE
1. Never invent a subjective report, objective finding, or measurement. If it is
   not in the transcript, it did not happen — leave it blank or mark "Not documented".
2. Never state a diagnosis. Suggest differentials and considerations only.
3. Never recommend medication, doses, or specific treatment protocols.
4. Everything generated is an "AI-Assisted Draft — Review Required".
5. Suggestions are prompts to the physio, kept clearly separate from the SOAP draft.
6. The physiotherapist edits, accepts, and signs off. The AI assists; it never decides.
